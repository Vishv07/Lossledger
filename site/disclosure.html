LossLedger / disclosure policy

Disclosure policy

LossLedger exists to find delivery bugs in other people's software. How those findings are handled decides whether this project is a contribution or a nuisance.

What counts as a finding

A MISSING or DUP_BUG result against a named collector or store version that reproduces 10 out of 10 times from a committed scenario, on default or explicitly stated configuration.

Anything that does not reproduce 10 out of 10 is not a finding. Scenarios whose verdicts vary across replays are labelled FLAKY, reported separately, and never published.

The process

  1. Maintainers first, privately. The security contact if the project designates one, otherwise the issue tracker. The report includes the scenario, the seed, the pinned versions, and a one-command reproduction.
  2. 14 days, or until fixed or acknowledged, whichever is sooner. During that window the public matrix shows the cell as "1 issue under disclosure" without the detail.
  3. ORDER and LATE findings may be filed publicly straight away. They are bugs, not hazards.
  4. Configuration defaults are described as defaults. A finding about a default cooldown is reported as "default-config behaviour", never as "X is broken", and the tuned-config result is shown beside it.
  5. Re-test posts after a fix are mandatory. They are the half of the loop that builds the alliance.

Who publishes

The owner files and publishes. No exceptions. An implementing agent drafts findings and stops there.

Tone

Reports praise what passed as loudly as what failed. Methodology first, findings second, zero gloating. A report that only lists failures is marketing, not measurement.

Reporting a vulnerability in LossLedger itself

Open a draft security advisory on the repository, or contact the owner privately. Please do not open a public issue first.

The emitter writes files only inside directories you configure, resolving that root through symlinks once at startup and refusing every path that escapes it. The verifier is read-only against your stores by construction: the sink interface exposes no write method. Neither component requires root.

The shared signing key keeps foreign or forged lines out of the ledger. It is not a confidentiality boundary, and the truncated signature on each line is not sized to be one. It is read from the environment rather than a config file, so it stays out of the files people paste into issues, and it never appears in metrics, reports or logs.
