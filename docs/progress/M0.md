# M0: validation spike

**Status: waived by the owner, not executed.**

## What M0 required

The PRD makes M0 a kill-gate rather than a formality. It asked for the smallest
honest probe (emitter, verifier, filesink, one real adapter, one collector in
compose) run for at least 48 hours continuously, with a stop-and-report rule if
the probe could not catch a known loss.

## Why it was waived

The implementing session could not satisfy it:

1. **48 hours of wall clock.** The soak is bound by elapsed time, not by effort.
   No amount of work inside one session produces a 48 hour continuous run.
2. **No real pipeline reachable.** The owner's ELK and ClickHouse infrastructure
   was not available to the session, and the Docker daemon on the build machine
   was not running.
3. **Platform mismatch.** The PRD targets Linux. The build machine is macOS, so
   journald and the disk-pressure injector could not be exercised even in
   principle.

The owner was shown these three constraints and chose to waive M0 and proceed
to M1, which needs no Docker and no external store.

## What this costs

M0 existed because of the demand caveat in PRD section 2.4: the audience for
this tool is narrower than the GitHub issue trail suggests. Waiving it means
**demand remains unproven on the owner's own infrastructure.** That question is
deferred, not answered.

What M0 would have tested and M1 does not:

- Whether a real collector, on real traffic, loses anything worth reporting
- Whether the always-green proof is itself worth having to the owner
- Whether canary volume is tolerable in the owner's billed stores

## What replaces it, partially

M1's self-validation gate covers the third M0 acceptance path, "a deliberately
misconfigured collector is caught exactly", against a reference pipeline rather
than a real one. refbad drops a seeded range, duplicates another and truncates
multi-line events, and the gate requires the verifier to report exactly those
ranges. That proves the core concept is not broken. It does not prove anyone
wants it.

**Before any public release, M0 should be run for real.** The owner should
point the emitter at a staging pipeline for 48 hours and record the verdict
here.
